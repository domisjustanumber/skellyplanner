# Handoff: FreeMoCap room planner (was Mocap Lens Calculator)

State as of 17 Nov 2026 (thirteenth change): a local Vite project, run with `npm run dev`; no published copy (the claude.ai Artifact, last at version 4, is retired). Picks up from a claude.ai planning session; the dated changes below are newest first.

## The project

Planning a low-cost markerless motion capture rig:

- **Cameras:** free-running USB 2.0 UVC, cheap dedicated webcam bridge chips, M12 lenses, fixed IR-cut filters, 38 mm boards.
- **Sensors:** AR0234 (global shutter, 1920×1200, 3.0 µm, colour or mono) and OV9281 (global shutter, mono, 1280×800, 3.0 µm).
- **Pose models:** YOLO26, RTMW, MediaPipe. Crop heights: 256 px (RTMPose, MediaPipe) and 384 px (larger RTMW).
- **Stock M12 focal lengths offered:** 2.8, 3.6, 6.0 mm (the classic CCTV set; cut from 2.1, 2.5, 2.8, 3.2, 3.6, 4.0, 6.0 on 17 Nov 2026, see the note below). Earlier notes and `research/` still name the dropped lenses.
- **Goal:** the cheapest setup for any volume size, ranked by camera count (no prices), with a pixel floor for the pose model. Settle on one lens per sensor so scaling up is just adding cameras, and the remaining choice is which sensor.

## How we got here

1. **Single-distance calculator** (the "Single camera" mode until 17 Nov 2026, then removed): focal-length window between `f_min = crop·p·D_far / H` (enough pixels at far) and `f_max = sensor_vertical·D_near / coverage` (full body at near).
2. **Fixed 4-camera volume mode** (built, then replaced): one camera centred on each side of an L×L square at standoff s. Max L at s = 1.5/2.0/2.5/3.0 m came out as 2.3/3.1/3.7/3.9 m (OV9281, 256 crop) and 3.8/4.3/4.7/5.0 m (AR0234, 256). AR0234 at 384 matches OV9281 at 256. These are kept as a regression check in `tests/engine.test.js`.
3. **Read two papers** (below). Samani et al. showed pairs about 45° apart work best and pairs past about 90° do poorly, so a 4-camera square with 90° neighbours and 180° opposites was questionable.
4. **Room planner** (current default) with these owner decisions:
   - First two cameras at 0° (front) and 45°. The rest are **spread evenly** over the remaining 315° (`layout()` in `engine.js`).
   - **Partial coverage is OK.** Cameras don't each need to see the whole volume, so the count grows with volume size.
   - **Room size is an input.** Cameras stand between the volume edge and the wall.
   - **Cheapest means fewest cameras**, compared side by side for the two sensors. No unit prices.
5. **Orientation is automatic** (latest change). The room planner tries every lens in both orientations; single-camera mode (removed 17 Nov 2026) used landscape when it fit and portrait only when landscape could not.

## The model (src/engine.js)

**When a camera sees a floor point.** A point (x, y) is seen by a camera when:
- depth along the lens axis z satisfies `near ≤ z ≤ far`, where
  - `near = cov·f / (pxV·p)`: a full body (vertical coverage, default 2.2 m) fits;
  - `far = H·f / (crop·p)`: the person is at least `crop` px tall (H = 1.8 m);
- the point is inside the horizontal field of view, so its lateral offset is at most `z·pxH·p / (2f)`.

**When a point counts as covered.** It needs at least two cameras that see it, with the angle between them measured at the point inside [minA, maxA] (default 30–90°). Coverage is the share of a 25×25 grid over the volume (13×13 in the sweep). The default target is 100%.

**Where cameras stand.** Each camera sits on the ray from the volume centre at its layout angle, aimed at the centre. Its distance along the ray is chosen independently per camera, in 0.1 m steps from the volume boundary to the wall (minus 5 cm), to maximise how many grid points it sees on its own. This is greedy and doesn't optimise pairs jointly.

**How the camera count is found.** `solve()` tries n = max(2, minN)…12 and returns the first n that meets the coverage target. The layout changes with n, so coverage isn't strictly monotonic in n.

**How one lens per sensor is picked** (until the ninth change; the sweep now only fills the comparison table, and Auto plans every lens, see that note). `sweep()` runs square volumes from 1.5 m up to (shorter room side − 1 m) in 0.5 m steps, for every lens and both orientations. `pickLens()` ranks rows by most sizes solved, then fewest total cameras, then the longer lens, then landscape. A sweep stops early for a lens once it fails at a size. The page caches the sweep per room and coverage rule.

**Key insight from the maths.** For a fixed lens, `far/near = H·pxV / (cov·crop)`, which doesn't depend on f. In landscape that's about 3.8× for the AR0234 and 2.6× for the OV9281 at a 256 crop, and 2.6× and 1.7× at 384. The lens only slides the band nearer or farther, which is why the best lens depends on room size. The far limit is the same for both sensors on the same lens.

## The two papers

**Samani et al., "Optimizing camera placement in multi-view markerless motion capture…", BMC Research Notes 19:303 (2026), doi:10.1186/s13104-026-07886-4.**
- **Setup:** 10 men; 8 consumer cameras at 15 fps, about 3 m from the subject, mostly at about 1 m high; 6 two-camera stereo pairs. MediaPipe Pose (complexity 1) compared against Qualisys.
- **Best pair:** about 45° apart (cameras 1–3), with static MAE 9.28°, ICC 0.851 and dynamic lifting MAE 12.92°, ICC 0.823.
- **Worst pair:** about 135° apart (3–7), with ICC around 0.3 and about 2.5–3× the error.
- **Second best:** cameras 1–2, which are stacked vertically (1.0 m and 1.6 m) with almost no horizontal angle, reached ICC 0.80 static. That points to consistent detection mattering more than baseline width.
- **Their explanation:** the "detection constraint". Models trained mostly on frontal views miss keypoints from side-on angles.
- **Limits:** only two angles are stated explicitly, so there's no curve. Opposite pairs weren't tested because they couldn't share a calibration view. The "neck" isn't C7, so neck agreement was poor.

**Walker et al., "The effect of camera configuration on multi-camera markerless motion capture for biomechanical analysis", J. Biomech. 206:113522 (2026), doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2026.113522.**
- **Setup:** 18 Sony RX0II at 1920×1080, 120 Hz, 1.6 m high, every 20° round an ellipse. 10 × 8 m and 6 × 4 m volumes; 18/12/6-camera subsets; Theia3D.
- **Results:** kinematics changed less than 2–4° between configurations. In the 6 × 4 m volume, 6 cameras failed to track all segments; the trunk was lost at the top of a jump. Tracking also failed near the volume edges where coverage was thin. The authors conclude geometry and coverage matter more than camera count.
- **Limits:** no marker reference, and the subsets reused the 18-camera calibration. The authors have Theia funding ties.

## Open questions and known gaps

1. **Fisheye and distortion (pending owner choice).** Every lens is treated as rectilinear. Cheap short M12 lenses are often strongly barrel-distorted or true fisheye, so real coverage is wider and edge pixels fewer. Many 2.1 mm lenses are made for 1/4"–1/3" sensors and won't cover the AR0234's 6.8 mm diagonal. Three options were offered:
   1. A max diagonal FOV or minimum-lens guard per sensor.
   2. An editable lens list with each lens's stated FOV and image circle, used instead of the rectilinear FOV. Recommended once real candidate lenses are known.
   3. A barrel-distortion factor.

   (Moot since the single-camera mode was removed on 17 Nov 2026.) The "near fisheye" flag in single-camera mode (diagonal over 120°) never fires with the stock list, because the rectilinear diagonal tops out around 117°.
2. **The 30° lower bound** of the pair-angle band is a judgement call. Samani only supports about 45° being good and wider than 90° being poor.
3. **Subject facing** is covered by the turning arc. Spare cameras: see the fourth and fifth changes of 16 Nov; the page reports what stays covered with one view blocked but does not require it, and spends cameras on the back and hands behind it first.
4. **2D only.** No camera height, one subject; self-occlusion only as the back (hands behind it), other occlusion only as one blocked 30° slice of views. Hands in front of the body hidden from rear cameras, arms hiding each other, and the other hand are not modelled. Corners are only checked through the grid.
5. **Greedy placement.** Cameras are now chosen from wall positions one mirrored pair at a time with one repair pass. It is not an exact minimum.
6. **Sweep assumptions.** The sweep assumes a lens that fails at one size fails at all larger sizes, and it uses square volumes only.
7. **Performance.** Since the ninth change Auto plans six lens and orientation pairs per sensor: 2–5.4 s per new input in Chrome, over the 1.5 s / 4 s targets (the tenth change took out the size search: 8x8 / 6x6 at 90 degrees now 3.6 s). Plans are cached, so the slider and lens pickers stay instant.
